why: "Our community group's channels are the only place some members encounter the group. For the people who don't come to meetings the channels are the DCCG. \n\nRight now what happens can be a bit random. There is no line, so there is no crossing it, so there is nothing for anyone to be accountable for. That has two consequences \n1. - the posts themselves can sometimes be personal attacks or skewed representations of what the group stands for\n2. - some members who read them decide this isn't a group they want to be a part of, and go quiet or leave."

  poor: "These are measured a year after the policy is in force:\nA policy exists. Nobody has read it, nobody has applied it, and the channels are exactly as they were."
  expected: "These are measured a year after the policy is in force:\nWhen a post crosses the line, someone acts, and what they did is what the policy said they would do. The action is about the post."
  good: "These are measured a year after the policy is in force:\nFewer posts cross the line, because members know what the channel is for before they write. The policy is doing its work upstream of enforcement."
  exceptional: "These are measured a year after the policy is in force:\nMembers who had gone quiet are posting again, and hard subjects — 1080, roading, money — are argued respectfully in the channel without anyone being driven out of it. The group can disagree with itself in public and stay a group."
planks:
  - name: "Channels, and what each one is for"
    purpose: "Name exactly which channels the policy governs, and state what each is for in the same breath."
    why: "A policy that says \"our communications\" governs nothing. The Facebook page, the group chat, the noticeboard, emails from the committee, and what a member says at a meeting are five different things with five different levels of group responsibility.\nA channel with a stated purpose can be objectively moderated by anyone — this isn't the place for that — which is a judgement about the post not the poster."
    outcomes:
      poor: "\"Our communications\" — undefined, and every future dispute starts with an argument about jurisdiction."
      expected: "The official channels are listed, each with a plain sentence saying what it's for."
      good: "Each channel also names who owns it. Where a channel is for discussion rather than notices, it says so — the group needs at least one channel where members can argue safely."
      exceptional: "The list has a rule for adding channels and for changing a channel's purpose, so the policy survives both the next platform and the next disagreement about what a channel is for."
    irreversible: false
  - name: "What the policy must cover"
    purpose: "Set the minimum content the draft must address."
    why: "It's easy to write a policy that says be respectful, be kind, be community-minded. Nobody argues with it and nobody can use it. What we need is something that helps a person about to post something they're angry about, and the person reading a post that's gone too far.\n\nOnce a post is up, the damage is done. This policy should tell people what this channel is for, what it will and won't tolerate, and where to take something that doesn't belong in this channel.\n\nBeing off-topic and being disrespectful are two different things. Someone can post about something the channel is for and still go too far. 1080 is this community's business — a member arguing against it is using the channel exactly as intended. A member personally attacking or abusing another member is not."
    outcomes:
      poor: "A statement of values. Respect, kindness, community spirit.."
      expected: "It says what each channel is for and what will land badly, in plain enough terms that a member can check their own post against it before they send it. Off-topic and out-of-line are named as separate things."
      good: "It also tells someone where to take a post that doesn't belong in this channel, so being redirected isn't the same as being shut down. It says who speaks for the group and who doesn't, so members aren't taken as speaking for us all. And it says what the group will tell members and how often, so the obligations run both ways."
      exceptional: "Members use it on themselves without being asked. The hard subjects still get argued, and the arguing stays about the subject."
    irreversible: false
  - name: "Who drafts it"
    purpose: "Name the drafting group and its size."
    why: "A policy about how we speak to each other should encompass all the different positions  of the community"
    outcomes:
      poor: "One person writes it alone and brings it back. It is received as the their policy."
      expected: "Two or three members with a named convenor and a date."
      good: "The group includes at least one member who has been on the receiving end of the problem, and one who is sceptical the policy is needed."
      exceptional: "The sceptic is a co-author rather than a consultee, so the objections are inside the draft rather than waiting for it at the meeting."
    irreversible: false
  - name: "The boundaries"
    purpose: "Say what's out of bounds, channel by channel."
    why: "We all need to know how the boundaries for each channel are defined and what they mean.  Any subject can be raised. Some channels suit some messages and others others. The way things are communicated depends on the subject and the channel. The policy defines acceptable and unacceptable ways of communicating within each channel."
    outcomes:
      poor: "Ambiguous or ill defined boundaries allow boundaries to be crossed with no consequences"
      expected: "Boundaries are defined. Communication is able to be measured against the boundaries to understand if any have been crossed."
      good: "Boundaries are well defined and result in an absence of personal attacks, feuds or abuse."
      exceptional: "The boundaries become a crystal clear and result in communication becoming deeper, richer and more regenerative. From the outside, the way we communicate with each other is admired and modelled."
    irreversible: false
  - name: "The policy boundary"
    purpose: "This policy will sit below the disputes policy (when that's done). We need to know where this policy is applied or when something needs to be escalated."
    why: "We need to know what happens when a post goes too far and what happens next. Being a member of the DCCG is a right, it also carries some responsibilities."
    outcomes:
      poor: "When a boundary is crossed, no one knows what to do next"
      expected: "When a boundary is crossed, a procedure is followed and a resolution is achieved"
      good: "The boundaries are well defined. Everyone knows exactly what will happen if they are crossed. When they are crossed (bound to happen) the issue becomes about the boundary and not the person."
      exceptional: "The communications policy and the disputes policy (yet to be written) are complimentary and seamlessly work together to ensure communications are respectful and constructive and build community."
    irreversible: false
  - name: "Moderation, Duty of Care, and Admin Support"
    purpose: "The policy must explicitly establish the moderation structure, roles, and safety protocols for those administering the channels."
    why: "This can be uncomfortable work. Clear rules and escalation pathways are needed so moderators don't burn out."
    outcomes:
      poor: "The policy leaves moderation to whoever owns the account, with no guidelines on how or when they act."
      expected: "The policy names who moderates each channel, requires at least two admins per active discussion channel so no one acts in isolation, and sets out standard response steps."
      good: "The policy includes a mandatory step-back rule for moderators who are personally involved in a thread, and a clear process for temporarily pausing comments on a thread if abuse escalates past real-time moderation capacity."
      exceptional: "Moderation actions are consistent regardless of who is moderating, and the operational load on volunteers remains sustainable over time."

      poor: "The policy is never developed"
      expected: "The date for policy review is stated and agreed by the people developing it."
      good: "The policy is developed and refined quickly and efficiently"

That the Donnellys Crossing Community Group Incorporated commission the development of a Communications Policy.

The Policy shall state what each of the Group's channels is for, who administers it, and who may post to it; where the boundaries of what is acceptable or unacceptable lie, stated as conduct; who may act on a communication and within what time; what actions are available; how a member seeks review of an action they want taken or one that is taken against them; who may speak for the Group; and what the Committee undertakes to communicate to members and how often.
